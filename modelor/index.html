Modelor: Early 2D Resource & Operational Geometry Engine

The First Foundation: Combining Spatial Modeling, Operational Geometry & Volume Calculations (2007)

The Genesis: Bridging Geological Modeling & Operational Costing

Developed in 2007 as my first major engineering software project during my studies at Amirkabir University of Technology, Modelor was designed to address a critical domain gap: creating accessible, lightweight 2D grid-based modeling for tabular (layered) ore deposits without relying solely on monolithic commercial suites.

While standard commercial tools provided basic surface interpolation, field operations needed a direct link between geological surfaces, operational geometry (benches, ramps), and precise volume calculations for accurate extraction cost estimation. Modelor laid the architectural and domain foundation for my later work in offshore data platforms and modern Industrial AI engines[cite: 1].

Core Engineering & Analytical Capabilities

1. Spatial Interpolation & Data Fusion Pipeline

Modelor ingested exploration borehole datasets (Collar and Assay files) alongside digitized map data, standardizing coordinate and depth inputs[cite: 1]. It implemented an Inverse Distance to a Power (IDW) spatial interpolation engine to generate regularized 2D grid models across custom extents, cell sizes, and smoothing parameters[cite: 1].

2. Operational Geometry & Surfer Interoperability

In real-world deployment on a layered copper deposit, Modelor demonstrated hybrid data integration by importing Kriging-generated topographic and pit models from Surfer[cite: 1]. The platform layered active operational geometry—such as mining benches and haulage ramps—directly onto these models to compute real-world excavation volumes[cite: 1].

3. 3D Surface Rendering & Mathematical Volume Engine

Equipped with a native OpenGL rendering pipeline, Modelor visualized multi-layered 3D surfaces and interactive borehole traces[cite: 1]. It executed volume calculations between arbitrary surfaces (e.g., roof and floor layers) using the numerical trapezoidal rule, forming the quantitative core for operational cost estimation[cite: 1].

Technical Architecture & Stack (2007)

Designed for execution on resource-constrained hardware, Modelor prioritized low-level spatial computation performance[cite: 1]:

  • Core Engine & UI: Object Pascal / Delphi (engineered for standalone execution speed and UI responsiveness)[cite: 1].
  • Graphics & Visualization: Hardware-accelerated OpenGL for real-time 3D surface rendering and cross-section generation[cite: 1].
  • Data & File Handling: Proprietary binary grid formats with CSV/ASCII parsers for Collar, Assay, and Surfer Grid interchange[cite: 1].
  • Mathematical Algorithms: Custom IDW spatial estimation algorithms, polyline cross-section extraction, and trapezoidal numerical integration[cite: 1].

Validation & Verification

To verify numerical precision, Modelor's outputs were benchmarked against Golden Software Surfer using identical input data, grid resolutions, and cross-section vectors[cite: 1]:

Validation Metric Benchmark Result
Volume Calculation Accuracy Matched Surfer trapezoidal volume outputs under identical surface constraints[cite: 1].
Contour & Cross-Section Consistency Identical spatial trend lines and elevation profiles across arbitrary slice vectors[cite: 1].
Operational Impact Successfully computed bench-by-bench excavation volumes for financial feasibility[cite: 1].

Evolutionary Impact: From Spatial Geometry to Industrial AI

Modelor was my foundational project in domain-driven modeling[cite: 1]. The engineering principles established here—handling spatial geometry, volume computation, data validation, and tying physical parameters directly to financial/cost models—served as the direct blueprint for my subsequent career:

  • Offshore Fleet Operations: Expanding deterministic spatial rules into real-time telemetry processing for 70+ vessels.
  • Industrial AI (Plan2Cost / Cost2Plan): Transitioning classical 2D/3D spatial geometry and volume estimation into automated computer vision and hybrid ML cost engines[cite: 1].

Note: Source code and documentation for this early project have been compiled for retrospective analysis and are accessible on GitHub at modelor-tabular-deposit-modeling[cite: 1].
