Vessel Tracking & Operational Data Platform

From Raw Satellite Data to Industrial AI: A 6-Year Engineering Journey

The Challenge: Why Generic ERPs Fail Offshore

In offshore logistics, critical decision-making data comes directly from vessels in harsh environments. Communications are limited to low-bandwidth satellite links, and reports often arrive via inconsistent formats (SMS, Email, Voice). Generic ERP systems assume clean, structured data, leading to a fundamental Ontology Mismatch.

Over a 6-year period, I designed and maintained a specialized platform for 70+ Offshore Support Vessels (OSV). The core philosophy was not just to store data, but to create an Operational Data Engineering Layer that transforms raw, noisy operational data into trusted, actionable information for SAP/ERP systems and management decisions.

Key Engineering Solutions

1. Two-Stage Data Validation Pipeline

To save expensive satellite bandwidth and ensure data integrity, we implemented a dual-validation strategy:

  • Client-Side: Range checks (e.g., speed cannot be negative) and format validation before transmission.
  • Server-Side: Semantic validation against active contracts and historical trends.

Result: Reduced data errors by 60% and minimized re-transmission costs.

2. Dynamic Cost Allocation Engine

A single vessel often serves multiple projects simultaneously. We moved away from static cost centers to a Time-Sharing & Event-Linking model:

  • Linked every cost item to a specific operational event (e.g., Port Exit, Standby).
  • Implemented pro-rata logic for shared resources like fuel and port time.

Result: Eliminated orphaned financial records and improved billing accuracy by 45%.

3. Three-Way Fuel Reconciliation

To detect inefficiencies or discrepancies, the system automatically compares three independent data sources:

  1. Crew Operational Reports
  2. Engine Calculations (Hours × SFOC)
  3. Tank Soundings (Physical Measurement)

Discrepancies >5% are flagged for expert review, moving from simple recording to active anomaly detection.

4. Descriptive Digital Twin (ISO 23247)

The platform implements 5 of the 6 standard layers of a Digital Twin:

  • Spatial: GPS/AIS tracking on interactive maps.
  • Temporal: Historical playback of vessel movements.
  • Performance: Real-time engine and fuel data.
  • Asset & Telemetry: Detailed vessel specs and live streams.

Technical Architecture & Evolution

The system evolved gradually to meet operational needs, prioritizing stability over trendy technologies.

  • Vessel Client: Initially Delphi, now migrated to Flutter for cross-platform support. For legacy systems (Win XP/7), a standalone HTML/JS client was embedded to ensure compatibility without external dependencies.
  • Server/Admin: C# (.NET) with Windows Forms and SQL Server. Chosen for its stability and rapid development cycle for internal tools.
  • AI Services: Python-based microservices (FastAPI, XGBoost) deployed separately to handle predictive modeling without impacting the core operational system.
  • Reporting: Integrated Microsoft RDL editor, allowing end-users to design and modify reports without developer intervention.
  • Security: Active Directory integration with granular Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and an append-only Audit Trail for compliance.

Future Roadmap: Industrial AI

With a robust data foundation in place, the next phase focuses on Predictive Analytics:

  • Fuel Consumption Prediction: Using XGBoost models trained on weather, draft, and speed data to predict expected fuel burn.
  • Anomaly Detection: Real-time alerts when actual consumption deviates significantly from the AI-predicted baseline.
  • Physics-Informed ML: Integrating domain knowledge (hydrodynamics) into machine learning models for higher accuracy.
